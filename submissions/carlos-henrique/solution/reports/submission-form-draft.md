# JourneyGraph Submission Form Draft

## Project Name

JourneyGraph

## One-line Description

**Primary:** Governed Retention Intelligence from Temporal Customer Journeys.

**Short:** From fragmented SaaS events to governed, explainable customer journeys.

## Problem

**Primary:** Retention evidence is fragmented across accounts, subscriptions, usage, support, churn, and reactivation at incompatible grains. Direct joins can inflate totals, contradictory chronology can create impossible journeys, and a terminal churn flag can erase recurrence. Teams need a governed temporal reconstruction before they can decide what deserves investigation or testing.

**Short:** Fragmented grains, unsafe joins, and conflicting chronology make conventional retention views difficult to trust.

## Solution

**Primary:** JourneyGraph audits source relationships, builds a canonical temporal event log, preserves invalid records in quarantine, reconstructs customer journeys, mines stable sequences, and promotes supported evidence into a local graph. Deterministic queues organize evidence for human review, and an Experiment Lab classifies `UNTESTED` designs by readiness.

**Short:** A governed pipeline from audited temporal events to journeys, graph evidence, human review, and experiment readiness.

## Key Features

**Primary:** Canonical event log; explicit quarantine; MAIN/STRICT sensitivity; recurrent churn and reactivation journeys; stable pattern promotion; NetworkX-first JourneyGraph; seven explainable review queues; eight governed experiment designs; pt-BR dashboard; deterministic local demo.

**Short:** Temporal governance, journey mining, graph evidence, review queues, experiment design, and deterministic demo.

## Technical Approach

**Primary:** Python produces validated analytical artifacts and deterministic JSON snapshots. NetworkX is the reference graph runtime, with Neo4j export optional. A Next.js application statically prerenders evaluator routes and reads only local snapshots. Validation covers hashes, metric reconciliation, privacy, temporal cutoffs, links, tests, and product claims.

**Short:** Python + NetworkX analytical pipeline, deterministic JSON snapshots, and a statically prerendered Next.js demonstration.

## Use of AI

**Primary:** An AI coding assistant supported task decomposition, bounded implementation drafts, test scaffolding, alternatives, review, and documentation. Its suggestions were reviewed, corrected, tested, and revalidated before acceptance. The delivered product has no runtime AI dependency.

**Short:** AI-assisted development with human review, deterministic validation, and no runtime AI service.

## Human Judgment

**Primary:** Carlos Henrique rejected the mega-join, preserved temporal quarantine and recurrent churn, separated MAIN/STRICT populations, withheld unsupported Cox modeling, gated graph promotion, chose deterministic review queues, kept experiments `UNTESTED`, required human disposition, localized the interface, removed visible account keys, and corrected the evaluator build path.

**Short:** Eighteen documented decisions define the product's data, method, governance, privacy, and execution boundaries.

## Key Results

**Primary:** The fixed historical demo covers 500 anonymous accounts, 35,586 processed events, 13,927 usable events, 21,659 quarantined records, 4,221 journeys, 435 promoted patterns, 43 promoted transitions, 1,609 review items across seven queues, and eight experiment designs.

**Short:** 500 accounts, 13,927 usable events, 4,221 journeys, 435 patterns, seven queues, and eight `UNTESTED` designs.

## Business Value

**Primary:** Customer Success can investigate anonymous histories, Product can convert observations into falsifiable tests, leadership can see data-quality and readiness boundaries, and Data/AI teams can audit provenance and reproducibility. JourneyGraph reduces the risk of acting on inflated totals, invalid chronology, opaque rankings, or unsupported outcome claims.

**Short:** A defensible path from fragmented history to reviewable retention evidence and governed test planning.

## Governance

**Primary:** The product uses explicit temporal cutoffs, quarantine, MAIN/STRICT sensitivity, anonymous profiles, no displayed operational account IDs, descriptive graph semantics, deterministic rules, manual review, and `UNTESTED` experiment status. There is no automatic contact or intervention integration.

**Short:** Privacy-aware, cutoff-safe, non-causal, explainable, and human-reviewed by design.

## Limitations

**Primary:** Evidence is historical through December 31, 2024 and may not generalize. Quarantine is large, STRICT results can differ, graph structure is descriptive, associated MRR is contextual, queues are not forecasts, experiment designs have no observed effect, and the current demonstration is local rather than publicly deployed.

**Short:** Historical descriptive evidence; quality sensitivity, no prospective validation, no executed intervention, and no public deployment yet.
