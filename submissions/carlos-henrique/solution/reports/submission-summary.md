## 200 Words

JourneyGraph turns fragmented SaaS history into governed retention evidence. Accounts, subscriptions, usage, support, churn, and reactivation arrive at different grains; direct joins can multiply rows, timestamps can contradict lifecycle order, and a terminal churn flag can hide recurring episodes. JourneyGraph addresses those failures before presenting business conclusions.

The pipeline audits source relationships, builds a canonical event log, preserves invalid chronology in quarantine, and reconstructs customer journeys across explicit temporal scopes. Journey mining compares MAIN and STRICT quality populations, while the NetworkX-first JourneyGraph promotes only supported ROBUST or SENSITIVE patterns and transitions. Seven deterministic review queues attach evidence and discrete priority components to human investigation. Eight experiment designs specify eligibility, metrics, power, safeguards, and feasibility, but all causal statuses remain `UNTESTED`.

The validated local demonstration represents 500 anonymous accounts, 35,586 processed events, 13,927 usable events, 21,659 quarantined records, and 4,221 journeys. It runs from fixed local JSON snapshots with no external runtime service or credentials. The interface is pt-BR; submission materials are English-first. Privacy, non-causal language, temporal cutoffs, reproducibility, and human review are explicit. Internal documentation and technical gates are complete; recording, deployment, upload, Pull Request, form completion, and submission remain external user actions.
